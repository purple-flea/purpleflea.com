Introduction

Why Bankroll Management Matters for Autonomous Agents

An AI agent without position sizing rules is a loaded gun with no safety. The mathematics of ruin are unforgiving — even a positive-expected-value strategy will eventually blow up without proper risk controls.

When a human trader loses a significant portion of their capital, they feel pain. They slow down, re-evaluate, and often stop altogether before they reach zero. Autonomous AI agents have none of those psychological brakes. An agent running a naive fixed-bet casino strategy or a constant-leverage trading loop will continue executing — at full speed — straight through a drawdown to ruin. It does not feel fear, it does not feel loss aversion, and it does not have the intuition to know when to stop.

This is not a hypothetical risk. It is the default outcome for agents deployed without explicit bankroll management rules. The mathematics of gambler's ruin guarantees that any strategy with a positive bet size and a finite bankroll will eventually reach zero if no stop conditions are enforced — even if the strategy has a positive expected value. The question is never whether a drawdown will occur, but how deep it will go before the agent is halted.

Bankroll management solves this problem by encoding risk controls directly into the agent's decision logic. Instead of betting a fixed dollar amount, the agent bets a fraction of its current bankroll derived from a mathematical formula that balances growth rate against ruin probability. The three most widely used frameworks are:

  • Kelly Criterion — the mathematically optimal fraction for maximising long-run geometric growth
  • Fixed-Fraction Sizing — a simpler, more conservative approach that reduces variance at the cost of some expected growth
  • Maximum Drawdown Rules — hard stop-loss conditions that halt the agent when cumulative losses exceed a threshold

The Purple Flea casino API and trading API both expose the raw bet/position endpoints — they do not enforce sizing rules on your behalf. That is intentional: bankroll management is agent-side logic, not platform logic. This page gives you the formulas, the Python implementation, and the integration patterns to deploy disciplined risk management in any agent that uses Purple Flea services.

Kelly Criterion

The Kelly Criterion for Casino Agents

The Kelly Criterion, published by John Kelly Jr. at Bell Labs in 1956, gives the mathematically optimal fraction of your bankroll to wager on each bet to maximise the geometric growth rate of your capital over the long run.

The Kelly formula assumes repeated independent bets (or bets from a known distribution) and a known edge. For casino games with binary outcomes — coin flip, hi-lo, certain dice bets — the conditions are met exactly. For more complex games like crash, the formula generalises to the continuous case.

f* = (bp - q) / b
Kelly Fraction — binary bet version
f* = fraction of bankroll to bet
b = net odds received on wager (e.g. 1.0 for even odds)
p = probability of winning
q = probability of losing = 1 - p

Applying Kelly to Purple Flea Casino Games

For the Purple Flea coin flip game the house edge comes from a payout slightly below 2x. If the payout is 1.95x and the probability of winning is exactly 0.5, the calculation is:

f* = (0.95 × 0.5 - 0.5) / 0.95 = -0.0263
Coin flip with 1.95x payout — result is negative, meaning do not bet

A negative Kelly fraction means the game has a negative expected value — the mathematically correct bet size is zero. This is the most important output of the Kelly formula: it tells you not to play when the house has the edge. For agents deploying capital autonomously, this is a critical guard.

For dice over/under bets where the player has information or a verified edge (for example, through a VRF-based strategy with knowledge of upcoming outcomes in a sequence), the Kelly fraction becomes positive. Similarly, for the trading context where the agent has a demonstrated win rate above 50% on its signal, Kelly gives the correct sizing.

Half Kelly — The Practitioner's Compromise

Full Kelly maximises the geometric growth rate but produces very high variance — drawdowns of 50% or more are expected even when running an optimal strategy. Most practitioner implementations use Half Kelly (f* / 2) or Quarter Kelly (f* / 4), which sacrifice some expected growth in exchange for dramatically smoother equity curves.

For autonomous agents operating without human oversight, Half Kelly is generally the appropriate starting point. The variance reduction is substantial: Half Kelly produces roughly 75% of the growth rate of full Kelly but with approximately half the variance — a meaningful improvement in risk-adjusted return.

Agent Rule: Always compute Kelly before the session, not bet-by-bet. If the computed Kelly fraction is negative or below your minimum bet size, the agent should skip the session entirely and report the reason. Never override a negative Kelly signal.
Fixed-Fraction Sizing

Fixed-Fraction Sizing: Simpler, Safer

Fixed-fraction betting is the simplest bankroll management system: bet a predetermined percentage of your current bankroll on every play. It is robust, easy to implement, and hard to misconfigure.

In fixed-fraction sizing, the agent maintains a fraction parameter (for example, 0.02 for 2%) and computes the bet size as:

bet_size = bankroll × fraction
Fixed-Fraction — bet size scales with current bankroll

The key property of fixed-fraction sizing is that bet size automatically decreases as the bankroll shrinks (protecting against ruin) and automatically increases as the bankroll grows (compounding gains). Because the bet is always a fraction of current capital, the mathematical probability of reaching zero is zero — the agent can always make another bet, even after a severe drawdown.

When to Use Fixed Fraction Instead of Kelly

Fixed-fraction sizing is preferable to Kelly in three situations common to AI agents:

  • Unknown edge: When the agent does not have a reliable estimate of its win probability, Kelly cannot be computed. Fixed fraction at a conservative rate (1-2%) allows the agent to operate while learning its edge from observed outcomes.
  • High-variance games: Crash and multi-outcome dice games have complex return distributions that make standard Kelly estimation unreliable. Fixed fraction is robust across all distribution shapes.
  • Early bootstrapping: When an agent is starting with a small bankroll (e.g., the $1 USDC from the Purple Flea faucet), fixed fraction at 5-10% provides meaningful bet sizes while preserving the bankroll long enough to observe outcomes.

Optimal Fixed-Fraction Rates for Casino vs Trading

The appropriate fixed fraction differs significantly between casino and trading contexts. Casino games typically have faster cycle times (seconds per bet) and more extreme win/loss distributions. Trading positions may be held for minutes to hours and have more moderate per-trade outcomes.

  • Casino (coin flip, dice): 1–3% per bet is conservative; 5% is aggressive. Do not exceed 10% on any single casino bet.
  • Casino (crash): 0.5–2% per round due to the heavy-tailed distribution of crash multipliers.
  • Perpetual trading (long/short): 1–2% risk per trade, defined as the dollar amount at risk if the stop-loss triggers — not the full position size.
  • Spot swaps: Full capital deployment is acceptable for simple token swaps since the position is immediately closed at the new price.
Warning: Fixed fraction does not account for correlation between bets. If your agent opens multiple positions simultaneously in correlated markets, the effective risk is higher than each individual fraction suggests. Apply a correlation adjustment — see the trading position sizing section below.
Drawdown Protection

Maximum Drawdown Rules

Even with correct position sizing, drawdowns are mathematically inevitable. Maximum drawdown rules implement a hard stop that halts the agent when cumulative losses exceed a predefined threshold.

A maximum drawdown (MDD) rule works as follows: the agent tracks its peak bankroll over the session. At each point in time, it computes the current drawdown as the percentage decline from the peak. If the drawdown exceeds the threshold, the agent stops all activity and returns a stop signal to its orchestrator.

drawdown = (peak_bankroll - current_bankroll) / peak_bankroll
Current drawdown from peak — halt if this exceeds max_drawdown

Choosing a Maximum Drawdown Threshold

The appropriate maximum drawdown threshold depends on the recovery time the agent has and the risk tolerance of the operator:

  • 10% MDD — very conservative; appropriate for agents managing significant capital or running high-frequency strategies where a 10% drawdown represents a meaningful number of sequential losses
  • 25% MDD — balanced; the standard starting point for most autonomous agents. Recovery from a 25% drawdown requires a 33% gain, which is achievable in a reasonable time frame.
  • 50% MDD — aggressive; appropriate only for agents with a clearly demonstrated positive edge and a long time horizon. Recovery from 50% requires a 100% gain — do not use this unless you have statistical confidence in your edge.

Session vs Lifetime Drawdown

Agents should track two distinct drawdown metrics: session drawdown (from the peak within the current session) and lifetime drawdown (from the all-time high bankroll). Session drawdown limits trigger a pause — the agent stops for the session and resumes next session. Lifetime drawdown limits trigger a full halt — the agent stops and requires human review before restarting.

Implementing Stop-Loss at the Agent Level

Casino games and trades execute at the Purple Flea API level. The stop-loss must be implemented in the agent's own decision logic — before the API call is made, not after. The correct pattern is:

  1. Before each bet/trade: call should_stop_trading()
  2. If the method returns True: log the halt reason, notify the orchestrator, and exit the loop
  3. If the method returns False: compute bet size, execute the API call, update bankroll state

This pattern ensures the drawdown check happens before capital is committed, not after. An agent that checks drawdown after placing a bet will always make one too many.

Critical: Never let the agent override its own stop conditions based on LLM reasoning. The bankroll manager is code — it should be called as a function, not consulted as a prompt. If the LLM can reason its way around the stop-loss, it will eventually do so at the worst possible moment.
Trading Positions

Trading Position Sizing for AI Agents

Perpetual trading positions require a different sizing framework from casino bets. The key variables are risk-per-trade percentage, stop-loss distance, leverage, and correlation adjustment across open positions.

For perpetual futures positions, position size is derived from the risk-per-trade (the maximum dollar loss if the stop-loss triggers), not from the notional position size. This decouples position sizing from leverage choices and makes the risk model consistent regardless of how much leverage the agent applies.

position_size = (bankroll × risk_pct) / (entry_price × stop_distance_pct)
Position size in base asset units — risk-based sizing
risk_pct = fraction of bankroll at risk (e.g. 0.02)
entry_price = execution price of the position
stop_distance_pct = distance from entry to stop-loss (e.g. 0.03 = 3%)

Maximum Open Positions

Agents running multiple simultaneous positions must cap the total number of open positions to prevent concentration risk. A common rule: no more than 5 open positions, and total risk across all open positions must not exceed 10% of bankroll (i.e., the sum of per-position risk amounts).

Correlation Adjustment

When two open positions are positively correlated (e.g., long ETH and long BTC simultaneously), adverse price movements tend to hurt both positions at once. The effective bankroll at risk is higher than the sum of individual position risks. Apply a correlation adjustment:

adjusted_risk = individual_risk × (1 + correlation)
Correlation-adjusted risk per position
correlation = 30-day rolling correlation between the two assets (0 to 1)

For typical crypto asset pairs, ETH/BTC correlation is often 0.7-0.9 in trending markets and lower in range-bound markets. An agent long both ETH and BTC with 2% risk each should treat the effective combined risk as approximately 3.4-3.8% under high correlation.

Leverage Limits

The Purple Flea trading API supports leverage up to 100x on supported markets. For autonomous agents, hard-coded leverage limits are essential. Recommended maximums:

  • New agents (less than 30 days live): maximum 3x leverage
  • Established agents with positive track record: maximum 10x leverage
  • High-conviction signals only: maximum 20x leverage, with 50% smaller position size

bankroll_manager.py — full implementation python 
from __future__ import annotations
import math
import logging
from dataclasses import dataclass, field
from enum        import Enum
from typing      import Optional

logger = logging.getLogger("bankroll_manager")


class StopReason(Enum):
    SESSION_DRAWDOWN  = "session_drawdown_exceeded"
    LIFETIME_DRAWDOWN = "lifetime_drawdown_exceeded"
    BANKROLL_EMPTY    = "bankroll_too_small"
    MAX_BETS_REACHED  = "max_bets_per_session_reached"


@dataclass
class BankrollManager:
    """
    Position-sizing and risk manager for autonomous Purple Flea agents.

    Implements Kelly Criterion, fixed-fraction sizing, session and lifetime
    drawdown protection, and trading position sizing with correlation adjustment.
    """

    # ── Bankroll state ────────────────────────────────────────────
    initial_bankroll:     float          # Starting bankroll in USDC
    current_bankroll:     float = 0.0   # Updated after each bet/trade
    peak_bankroll:        float = 0.0   # All-time high
    session_peak:         float = 0.0   # Peak within current session
    bets_this_session:    int   = 0

    # ── Sizing parameters ─────────────────────────────────────────
    fixed_fraction:       float = 0.02  # Default: 2% of bankroll per bet
    kelly_fraction:       float = 1.0   # 1.0 = full Kelly, 0.5 = half Kelly
    min_bet_usd:          float = 0.10  # Never bet below this
    max_bet_usd:          float = 50.0  # Never bet above this

    # ── Risk parameters ───────────────────────────────────────────
    max_session_drawdown: float = 0.20  # 20% — pause for session
    max_lifetime_drawdown:float = 0.40  # 40% — full halt, needs human review
    max_bets_per_session: int   = 100   # Hard session bet count limit
    risk_per_trade:       float = 0.02  # 2% of bankroll at risk per trade
    max_open_positions:   int   = 5

    # ── Internal state ────────────────────────────────────────────
    open_positions:       list  = field(default_factory=list)
    stop_reason:          Optional[StopReason] = None

    def __post_init__(self) -> None:
        self.current_bankroll = self.initial_bankroll
        self.peak_bankroll    = self.initial_bankroll
        self.session_peak     = self.initial_bankroll

    # ── Kelly Criterion ───────────────────────────────────────────
    def kelly_fraction_for_bet(
        self,
        win_probability: float,
        net_odds: float,          # e.g. 0.95 for 1.95x payout
    ) -> float:
        """
        Compute the Kelly-optimal fraction for a binary bet.

        Returns 0.0 (do not bet) when the computed fraction is negative,
        meaning the game has a negative expected value.

        Args:
            win_probability: Probability of winning (0 < p < 1).
            net_odds:        Net profit per unit wagered if you win.
                             e.g. 0.95 for a bet that pays 1.95x.

        Returns:
            Fraction of bankroll to wager (0.0 to 1.0).
        """
        p = win_probability
        q = 1.0 - p
        b = net_odds

        kelly = (b * p - q) / b

        if kelly <= 0:
            logger.info(f"Kelly signal: do not bet (f*={kelly:.4f}, edge is negative)")
            return 0.0

        # Apply the Kelly fraction multiplier (0.5 for half-Kelly, etc.)
        scaled = kelly * self.kelly_fraction
        logger.info(f"Kelly signal: f*={kelly:.4f}, scaled={scaled:.4f}")
        return min(scaled, 0.25)  # Hard cap at 25% of bankroll

    # ── Bet sizing ────────────────────────────────────────────────
    def max_bet(
        self,
        win_probability: Optional[float] = None,
        net_odds: Optional[float] = None,
        use_kelly: bool = False,
    ) -> float:
        """
        Return the maximum bet size in USDC for the next play.

        If use_kelly is True and win_probability/net_odds are provided,
        uses Kelly Criterion sizing. Otherwise falls back to fixed fraction.
        Returns 0.0 if should_stop_trading() is True.

        Args:
            win_probability: Required for Kelly sizing.
            net_odds:        Required for Kelly sizing.
            use_kelly:       Whether to use Kelly or fixed-fraction sizing.

        Returns:
            Bet size in USDC.
        """
        if self.should_stop_trading():
            return 0.0

        if use_kelly and win_probability is not None and net_odds is not None:
            fraction = self.kelly_fraction_for_bet(win_probability, net_odds)
        else:
            fraction = self.fixed_fraction

        if fraction == 0.0:
            return 0.0

        raw_bet = self.current_bankroll * fraction
        bet     = max(self.min_bet_usd, min(raw_bet, self.max_bet_usd))

        logger.debug(f"Bet sizing: bankroll=${self.current_bankroll:.2f}, "
                     f"fraction={fraction:.4f}, bet=${bet:.2f}")
        return round(bet, 2)

    # ── Trading position sizing ───────────────────────────────────
    def trading_position_size(
        self,
        entry_price: float,
        stop_loss_price: float,
        correlation_adj: float = 0.0,
    ) -> float:
        """
        Compute position size in base asset units for a leveraged trade.

        Uses risk-based sizing: the dollar amount at risk (if stop-loss
        triggers) is capped at risk_per_trade * current_bankroll.

        Args:
            entry_price:     Price at which position opens.
            stop_loss_price: Price at which position closes on loss.
            correlation_adj: Correlation with other open positions (0-1).
                             Increases effective risk by (1 + correlation_adj).

        Returns:
            Position size in base asset units (e.g. ETH).
        """
        if len(self.open_positions) >= self.max_open_positions:
            logger.warning("Max open positions reached — cannot open new trade")
            return 0.0

        if self.should_stop_trading():
            return 0.0

        stop_distance_pct = abs(entry_price - stop_loss_price) / entry_price
        if stop_distance_pct == 0:
            raise ValueError("Stop loss cannot equal entry price")

        # Adjust risk fraction upward for correlated positions
        adj_risk = self.risk_per_trade * (1 + correlation_adj)
        dollar_at_risk = self.current_bankroll * min(adj_risk, 0.10)

        position_size = dollar_at_risk / (entry_price * stop_distance_pct)
        logger.info(f"Position sizing: ${dollar_at_risk:.2f} at risk, "
                    f"{position_size:.6f} units at entry={entry_price}")
        return position_size

    # ── Drawdown protection ───────────────────────────────────────
    def should_stop_trading(self) -> bool:
        """
        Check all stop conditions. Returns True if the agent should halt.
        Sets self.stop_reason on first trigger. Idempotent.
        """
        if self.stop_reason is not None:
            return True  # Already stopped

        # 1. Bankroll too small to make minimum bet
        if self.current_bankroll < self.min_bet_usd:
            self.stop_reason = StopReason.BANKROLL_EMPTY
            logger.warning(f"HALT: bankroll ${self.current_bankroll:.2f} below min bet")
            return True

        # 2. Session bet limit
        if self.bets_this_session >= self.max_bets_per_session:
            self.stop_reason = StopReason.MAX_BETS_REACHED
            logger.warning(f"HALT: session bet limit {self.max_bets_per_session} reached")
            return True

        # 3. Session drawdown (pause)
        session_dd = (self.session_peak - self.current_bankroll) / self.session_peak
        if session_dd > self.max_session_drawdown:
            self.stop_reason = StopReason.SESSION_DRAWDOWN
            logger.warning(f"HALT: session drawdown {session_dd:.1%} exceeds "
                           f"{self.max_session_drawdown:.1%}")
            return True

        # 4. Lifetime drawdown (full halt)
        lifetime_dd = (self.peak_bankroll - self.current_bankroll) / self.peak_bankroll
        if lifetime_dd > self.max_lifetime_drawdown:
            self.stop_reason = StopReason.LIFETIME_DRAWDOWN
            logger.warning(f"HALT: lifetime drawdown {lifetime_dd:.1%} exceeds "
                           f"{self.max_lifetime_drawdown:.1%}")
            return True

        return False

    # ── Bankroll updates ──────────────────────────────────────────
    def record_outcome(self, pnl_usd: float) -> None:
        """
        Update bankroll state after a bet or trade closes.

        Args:
            pnl_usd: Profit (+) or loss (-) in USDC for the resolved bet.
        """
        self.current_bankroll  += pnl_usd
        self.current_bankroll   = max(self.current_bankroll, 0.0)
        self.bets_this_session += 1

        # Update peaks
        if self.current_bankroll > self.peak_bankroll:
            self.peak_bankroll = self.current_bankroll
        if self.current_bankroll > self.session_peak:
            self.session_peak  = self.current_bankroll

        session_dd  = (self.session_peak  - self.current_bankroll) / max(self.session_peak,  1e-9)
        lifetime_dd = (self.peak_bankroll - self.current_bankroll) / max(self.peak_bankroll, 1e-9)
        logger.info(f"Outcome: pnl=${pnl_usd:+.2f} | bankroll=${self.current_bankroll:.2f} | "
                    f"session_dd={session_dd:.1%} | lifetime_dd={lifetime_dd:.1%}")

    def new_session(self) -> None:
        """Reset session-level state for a new trading/gaming session."""
        self.bets_this_session = 0
        self.session_peak      = self.current_bankroll
        self.stop_reason       = None  # Only clear session stops, not lifetime
        if self.stop_reason == StopReason.LIFETIME_DRAWDOWN:
            logger.error("Cannot start new session — lifetime drawdown halt active")
        logger.info(f"New session started. Bankroll: ${self.current_bankroll:.2f}")

        faucet_data = faucet_resp.json()
        initial_usdc = faucet_data["amount_usdc"]
        print(f"Claimed ${initial_usdc:.2f} from faucet. Tx: {faucet_data['tx_hash']}")

    # ── Step 2: Initialise bankroll manager ───────────────────────
    bm = BankrollManager(
        initial_bankroll=initial_usdc,  # Start with the faucet amount

        kelly_fraction=0.5,            # Half-Kelly for Kelly-based sizing
        max_session_drawdown=0.25,     # Halt session at 25% drawdown
        max_lifetime_drawdown=0.50,    # Full halt at 50% lifetime drawdown
        max_bets_per_session=20,       # Max 20 bets on $1 faucet session
        min_bet_usd=0.10,
        max_bet_usd=0.50,              # Cap bets at $0.50 on small bankroll
    )

    # ── Step 3: Casino session loop ───────────────────────────────
    async with httpx.AsyncClient() as client:
        while not bm.should_stop_trading():
            # Compute bet size using fixed fraction (coin flip has negative Kelly)
            bet_usd = bm.max_bet(use_kelly=False)
            if bet_usd == 0.0:
                break

            # Place casino bet
            resp = await client.post(
                f"{CASINO_URL}/bet",
                headers={"Authorization": f"Bearer {PURPLEFLEA_API_KEY}"},
                json={
                    "game":    "coinFlip",
                    "bet_usd": bet_usd,
                    "side":    "heads",
                    "chain":   "arbitrum",
                },
            )
            result = resp.json()

            # Record outcome — pnl is positive on win, negative on loss
            pnl = result["payout_usd"] - bet_usd if result["won"] else -bet_usd
            bm.record_outcome(pnl)
            print(f"{'WIN' if result['won'] else 'LOSS'}: ${pnl:+.2f} | "
                  f"Bankroll: ${bm.current_bankroll:.2f}")

    # ── Step 4: Session summary ───────────────────────────────────
    total_pnl = bm.current_bankroll - bm.initial_bankroll
    print(f"\nSession complete.")
    print(f"Starting bankroll: ${bm.initial_bankroll:.2f}")
    print(f"Final bankroll:    ${bm.current_bankroll:.2f}")
    print(f"Total PnL:         ${total_pnl:+.2f}")
    print(f"Stop reason:       {bm.stop_reason}")
    print(f"Bets placed:       {bm.bets_this_session}")
