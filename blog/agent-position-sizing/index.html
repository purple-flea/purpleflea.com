Position Sizing for AI Agents: Kelly Criterion, Risk Parity, and Fractional Betting

The single biggest determinant of long-run trading performance is not your entry signal — it is how much you bet on each trade. Too small and you leave compounding gains on the table. Too large and a losing streak destroys your capital base before your edge has time to assert itself. This guide covers the mathematics of optimal position sizing for AI agents across casino, trading, and multi-income environments.

Why Position Sizing Determines Survival

Consider two agents with identical win rates (55%) and identical payoff ratios (1:1). Agent A bets 50% of its capital on each trade. Agent B bets 5%. After 100 trades:

  • Agent A faces a near-certain risk of ruin. A sequence of 5 consecutive losses — which occurs frequently over 100 trades — reduces its capital by 97%. The agent effectively cannot recover.
  • Agent B compounds steadily, ending with approximately 2.3x its starting capital despite the same market results.

This asymmetry — where aggressive sizing causes catastrophic loss while conservative sizing allows compounding — is the central insight of position sizing theory. The math is unforgiving: a 50% loss requires a 100% gain just to return to breakeven. A 90% loss requires a 900% gain. Over-sizing does not just reduce returns; it changes the mathematics of recovery entirely.

AI agents have a structural advantage here: they never experience the emotional pull that causes human traders to over-size positions after a winning streak or under-size after losses. An agent will execute its position sizing formula with perfect consistency — which means getting the formula right is the entire game.

Key insight: For AI agents operating at scale across many simultaneous positions, position sizing is not a tactical decision made per-trade. It is a strategic parameter embedded in the agent's core logic. Changing it requires a deliberate code update, not a momentary judgment call — which is exactly how it should work.

Four Position Sizing Methods

There are four foundational methods for agent position sizing, ranging from mathematically optimal to pragmatically conservative. Understanding all four — and knowing when to apply each — is essential for any agent developer.

Kelly Criterion

Full Kelly

Mathematically optimal fraction of capital to wager given known win rate and payoff ratio. Maximizes long-run geometric growth rate. Sensitive to estimation errors in win rate.

Fractional Kelly

Fractional Kelly (25-50%)

Use a fraction (typically 25% or 50%) of the full Kelly bet. Dramatically reduces variance and drawdown in exchange for modestly lower expected growth. The practical standard for most agents.

Risk Parity

Risk Parity

Allocate capital so each position contributes equally to portfolio volatility. Automatically gives smaller positions to volatile assets and larger positions to stable assets. Ideal for multi-asset agents.

Drawdown Constraint

Max Drawdown Constraint

Cap position size so the maximum realistic loss (entry to stop) never exceeds a fixed percentage of total capital. Simple, robust, and works well combined with the other methods.

Kelly Criterion: Optimal Bet Sizing

The Kelly Criterion, published by John L. Kelly Jr. at Bell Labs in 1956, gives the mathematically optimal fraction of capital to wager on a bet with known probability and payoff. It was originally derived for information theory applications and later applied to gambling and investing by Ed Thorp.

The Kelly formula for a binary bet (win or lose, no partial outcomes) is:

Kelly Criterion — Binary f* = p - (1 - p) / b

Where f* is the fraction of capital to wager, p is the probability of winning, and b is the net payoff ratio (how many units you win per unit wagered — e.g., b = 1.5 means you win $1.50 for each $1 risked).

For trading, where positions have a range of outcomes rather than binary win/lose, the generalized Kelly formula is:

Kelly Criterion — Generalized (Trading) f* = W / L - (1 - W) / R

Where W is the win rate (fraction of profitable trades), L is the loss rate (1 - W), and R is the win/loss ratio (average winning trade size divided by average losing trade size).

Worked Example: Agent with Known Edge

Win rate (W) 0.58 (58%)
Loss rate (L = 1 - W) 0.42 (42%)
Avg win / avg loss ratio (R) 1.4
Full Kelly: W/L - (1-W)/R 0.58/0.42 - 0.42/1.4 = 1.381 - 0.300
Full Kelly fraction f* = 1.081 → capped at practical max
Quarter Kelly (25% of f*) 0.25 * 1.081 = 0.270 = 27% of capital

Important: Full Kelly fractions above 1.0 (which occur when your edge is very strong) should be capped at a practical maximum — typically 25-40% of capital — regardless of what the formula says. The Kelly formula assumes you know your win rate precisely, which you never do in live markets. Over-betting based on an overestimated win rate is far more dangerous than under-betting.

Kelly for Casino Agents

The Kelly Criterion applies directly to the Purple Flea Casino. Each game has a known expected value and payout structure, making Kelly sizing straightforward to calculate. For a coin flip with 50/50 odds but a 1.05x payout on wins:

Coin Flip Kelly Example f* = 0.50 - (1 - 0.50) / 1.05 = 0.024

Kelly says to bet 2.4% of bankroll per coin flip. At 25% fractional Kelly: 0.6% per flip. This may seem conservative, but it is the size that maximizes long-run wealth accumulation without risking ruin through variance.

Risk Parity: Volatility-Proportional Allocation

Risk parity is a portfolio construction technique that allocates capital not based on dollar amounts but based on volatility contributions. The goal: each position in the portfolio contributes an equal amount to total portfolio variance.

The intuition: a volatile asset (like a small-cap token) and a stable asset (like a USDC lending position) should not receive the same capital allocation. Giving them equal dollar allocations means the volatile asset dominates portfolio risk. Risk parity corrects for this by giving smaller positions to volatile assets and larger positions to stable ones.

The risk parity weight for asset i in a portfolio of N assets is:

Risk Parity Weight w_i = (1 / σ_i) / ∑(1 / σ_j)

Where σ_i is the realized volatility of asset i (typically measured as the annualized standard deviation of daily or hourly returns over a rolling lookback window).

Risk parity is particularly valuable for agents active across the full Purple Flea service suite — allocating capital between casino play (high variance per session, low correlation to crypto markets), trading positions (moderate variance, correlated to crypto), and escrow income (low variance, near-constant).

Python PositionSizer: Full Implementation

The following PositionSizer class implements all four sizing methods in a unified, async-compatible interface designed for live agent use with the Purple Flea Wallet API for dynamic capital lookups.

position_sizer.py Python 
import asyncio
import aiohttp
import numpy as np
from dataclasses import dataclass
from typing import Dict, List, Optional, Tuple
import logging

logger = logging.getLogger("position_sizer")

WALLET_API = "https://api.purpleflea.com/wallet"


@dataclass
class SizingResult:
    """Result of a position sizing calculation."""
    method:          str
    position_usd:    float   # dollar amount to deploy
    pct_of_capital:  float   # fraction of total capital
    kelly_fraction:  Optional[float]  = None
    risk_parity_wt:  Optional[float]  = None
    notes:           str = ""


class PositionSizer:
    """
    Multi-method position sizing for AI trading agents.
    Fetches live capital from Purple Flea Wallet API.
    Implements: Kelly, Fractional Kelly, Risk Parity,
                Max Drawdown Constraint.
    """

    def __init__(
        self,
        api_key:           str,
        kelly_fraction:    float = 0.25,   # 25% = quarter Kelly
        max_position_pct:  float = 0.15,   # never exceed 15% of capital
        min_reserve_usd:   float = 50.0,    # always keep this in wallet
        drawdown_limit:    float = 0.10,    # halt new trades at 10% dd
    ):
        self.api_key          = api_key
        self.kelly_fraction   = kelly_fraction
        self.max_position_pct = max_position_pct
        self.min_reserve_usd  = min_reserve_usd
        self.drawdown_limit   = drawdown_limit
        self._session:        Optional[aiohttp.ClientSession] = None
        self._peak_capital:   Optional[float] = None

    # ------------------------------------------------------------------ #
    # Public sizing methods                                             #
    # ------------------------------------------------------------------ #

    async def kelly_size(
        self,
        win_rate:     float,
        win_loss_ratio: float,
        symbol:       str = "",
    ) -> SizingResult:
        """
        Full Kelly + fractional Kelly position size.

        Args:
            win_rate:       fraction of trades that are profitable (0–1)
            win_loss_ratio: avg winning trade / avg losing trade size
            symbol:         asset symbol for logging

        Returns SizingResult with dollar amount to deploy.
        """
        capital = await self._get_available_capital()

        # Kelly formula for trading: W/L - (1-W)/R
        loss_rate    = 1 - win_rate
        full_kelly   = (win_rate / loss_rate) - (loss_rate / win_loss_ratio)
        full_kelly   = max(0.0, full_kelly)  # no negative sizing

        # Apply fractional Kelly
        frac_kelly   = full_kelly * self.kelly_fraction

        # Cap at max_position_pct
        final_frac   = min(frac_kelly, self.max_position_pct)
        position_usd = capital * final_frac

        logger.info(
            "%s Kelly: full=%.3f frac=%.3f final=%.3f → $%.2f",
            symbol, full_kelly, frac_kelly, final_frac, position_usd
        )

        return SizingResult(
            method         = "fractional_kelly",
            position_usd   = position_usd,
            pct_of_capital = final_frac,
            kelly_fraction = full_kelly,
            notes = (
                f"full_kelly={full_kelly:.3f} "
                f"frac_kelly={frac_kelly:.3f} "
                f"capital=${capital:.2f}"
            ),
        )

    async def risk_parity_weights(
        self,
        assets:         List[str],
        returns_matrix: Dict[str, List[float]],
    ) -> Dict[str, SizingResult]:
        """
        Compute risk-parity allocation weights across multiple assets.

        Args:
            assets:         list of asset symbols to allocate across
            returns_matrix: dict of {symbol: [list of period returns]}

        Returns dict of {symbol: SizingResult} with USD amounts.
        """
        capital = await self._get_available_capital()

        # Compute realized volatility for each asset
        vols: Dict[str, float] = {}
        for asset in assets:
            returns = returns_matrix.get(asset, [])
            if len(returns) < 2:
                logger.warning("Not enough returns for %s, skipping", asset)
                continue
            vols[asset] = float(np.std(returns, ddof=1)) + 1e-10

        if not vols:
            raise ValueError("No valid volatility data for any asset")

        # Inverse-volatility weights (risk parity core)
        inv_vols    = {a: 1.0 / v for a, v in vols.items()}
        total_inv   = sum(inv_vols.values())
        weights     = {a: iv / total_inv for a, iv in inv_vols.items()}

        results: Dict[str, SizingResult] = {}
        for asset, weight in weights.items():
            capped_wt   = min(weight, self.max_position_pct)
            position_usd = capital * capped_wt
            results[asset] = SizingResult(
                method          = "risk_parity",
                position_usd    = position_usd,
                pct_of_capital  = capped_wt,
                risk_parity_wt  = weight,
                notes = (
                    f"vol={vols[asset]:.4f} "
                    f"raw_weight={weight:.4f} "
                    f"capital=${capital:.2f}"
                ),
            )
            logger.info(
                "Risk parity %s: vol=%.4f weight=%.3f → $%.2f",
                asset, vols[asset], capped_wt, position_usd
            )

        return results

    async def fractional_kelly(
        self,
        win_rate:       float,
        win_loss_ratio: float,
        fraction:       Optional[float] = None,
        symbol:         str = "",
    ) -> SizingResult:
        """
        Convenience method: fractional Kelly with explicit fraction override.
        If fraction is None, uses self.kelly_fraction (default 0.25).
        """
        orig = self.kelly_fraction
        if fraction is not None:
            self.kelly_fraction = fraction
        result = await self.kelly_size(win_rate, win_loss_ratio, symbol)
        self.kelly_fraction = orig
        result.method = f"fractional_kelly_{fraction or orig:.0%}"
        return result

    async def max_drawdown_constraint(
        self,
        stop_pct:    float,
        risk_per_trade_pct: float = 0.02,
    ) -> SizingResult:
        """
        Size a position so the loss if stopped out equals
        risk_per_trade_pct of total capital.

        Args:
            stop_pct:          distance from entry to stop (as fraction, e.g. 0.03)
            risk_per_trade_pct: max fraction of capital to risk per trade (e.g. 0.02)

        Example: 3% stop, 2% capital risk → position = 2% / 3% = 66.7% of capital
                 But this is capped at max_position_pct anyway.
        """
        capital      = await self._get_available_capital()
        dollar_risk  = capital * risk_per_trade_pct
        position_usd = dollar_risk / stop_pct
        pct          = position_usd / capital
        pct_capped   = min(pct, self.max_position_pct)
        position_usd = capital * pct_capped

        logger.info(
            "DD constraint: stop=%.1f%% risk=%.1f%% → pos=$%.2f (%.1f%% of capital)",
            stop_pct * 100, risk_per_trade_pct * 100, position_usd, pct_capped * 100
        )

        return SizingResult(
            method         = "max_drawdown_constraint",
            position_usd   = position_usd,
            pct_of_capital = pct_capped,
            notes = (
                f"stop={stop_pct:.1%} risk_pct={risk_per_trade_pct:.1%} "
                f"uncapped_pct={pct:.1%} capital=${capital:.2f}"
            ),
        )

    async def is_drawdown_halted(self) -> Tuple[bool, float]:
        """
        Check if current drawdown exceeds limit.
        Returns (halted: bool, current_drawdown: float).
        """
        capital = await self._get_available_capital()

        if self._peak_capital is None:
            self._peak_capital = capital
            return False, 0.0

        self._peak_capital = max(self._peak_capital, capital)
        dd = (self._peak_capital - capital) / self._peak_capital

        if dd >= self.drawdown_limit:
            logger.warning(
                "Drawdown limit breached: %.1f%% >= %.1f%%",
                dd * 100, self.drawdown_limit * 100
            )
            return True, dd

        return False, dd

    # ------------------------------------------------------------------ #
    # Wallet API integration                                            #
    # ------------------------------------------------------------------ #

    async def _get_available_capital(self) -> float:
        """Fetch USDC balance from Purple Flea Wallet API."""
        async with self._session.get(
            f"{WALLET_API}/balance",
            headers={"Authorization": f"Bearer {self.api_key}"}
        ) as resp:
            data    = await resp.json()
            balance = float(data["usdc_balance"])

        available = max(0.0, balance - self.min_reserve_usd)
        logger.debug("Available capital: $%.2f (balance=$%.2f)", available, balance)
        return available

    async def __aenter__(self):
        self._session = aiohttp.ClientSession()
        return self

    async def __aexit__(self, *args):
        await self._session.close()

Fractional Kelly: The Practical Standard

Full Kelly is theoretically optimal, but it has a serious practical weakness: it assumes your estimate of win rate and payoff ratio is perfectly accurate. In reality, you are always working with estimates derived from limited historical data, and those estimates carry error.

The consequences of over-estimating your edge and using full Kelly are severe: because Kelly fraction is convex in the win rate, small over-estimates lead to dramatically over-sized positions and ruin-level drawdowns. This is not a theoretical concern — it has destroyed real trading systems operated by sophisticated quants.

Fractional Kelly addresses this by simply using a fraction of the full Kelly size — most commonly 25% (quarter Kelly) or 50% (half Kelly). The result is:

  • Dramatically lower variance and drawdown — quarter Kelly reduces variance to 6.25% of full Kelly variance
  • Robust to win rate estimation error — if you over-estimate your edge, the fractional bet is still survivable
  • Modestly lower expected growth — you sacrifice some long-run growth rate, but this is the correct tradeoff given estimation uncertainty

The growth rate penalty for fractional Kelly is smaller than most traders expect. For a 25% Kelly bet, you achieve approximately 75% of the maximum possible growth rate while taking on only 6.25% of the variance. This is an excellent tradeoff for any agent operating with real capital.

Comparing Kelly Fractions

Kelly Fraction % of Max Growth % of Max Variance Recommended For
Full Kelly (100%) 100% 100% Perfect edge estimation only
Half Kelly (50%) 75% 25% Well-tested strategies, lower uncertainty
Quarter Kelly (25%) 56% 6.25% Standard for live agent deployments
Tenth Kelly (10%) 29% 1% New agents, testing phases, high uncertainty

Best practice: Start new agents at tenth or quarter Kelly while your win rate estimate stabilizes over the first 50-100 trades. Increase toward half Kelly only after your live results closely match your backtested win rate for a sustained period.

Dynamic Sizing: Wallet-Responsive Position Adjustment

Static position sizing — fixing a dollar amount or percentage once and never changing it — ignores the most important variable in your sizing calculation: your current capital base. As your wallet grows, your absolute position sizes should grow proportionally. As your wallet shrinks after drawdowns, positions must shrink to prevent over-leveraging a depleted base.

The PositionSizer class handles this automatically by fetching the live Wallet API balance on every sizing call. This ensures every position is sized relative to your current actual capital, not some historical snapshot. The practical implementation in your agent loop looks like this:

Estimating Win Rate Accurately

Kelly sizing is only as good as your win rate estimate. A 5% over-estimate of win rate can more than double your Kelly fraction, dramatically increasing drawdown risk. Here are the key principles for reliable win rate estimation:

Sample Size Requirements

The standard error of a win rate estimate is sqrt(p*(1-p)/n) where n is the number of trades. For a true win rate of 55% and 50 trades, the standard error is approximately 7% — meaning your estimate could easily be 48% or 62%. At 200 trades, it drops to about 3.5%. At 500 trades, about 2.2%.

This has a direct implication: new agents should use tenth Kelly until they have at least 100-200 live trades. The computational cost of undersizing during the estimation period is far lower than the cost of a ruin-level drawdown caused by an overestimated win rate.

Regime Sensitivity

Win rates are not stable constants — they vary with market regime. A mean-reversion strategy might show a 60% win rate in ranging markets and a 35% win rate in trending markets. An agent that estimates its win rate from a backtest dominated by ranging conditions will dramatically over-size positions when the market begins to trend.

Best practice: estimate win rate over a rolling window (e.g., the last 30-50 trades) rather than the full trade history. This gives a regime-sensitive estimate that adapts as market conditions change.

Rolling win rate estimator Python 
from collections import deque

class RollingEdgeEstimator:
    """
    Maintains a rolling window estimate of win rate and win/loss ratio.
    Use this to feed PositionSizer.kelly_size() with adaptive estimates.
    """

    def __init__(self, window: int = 50, min_trades: int = 20):
        self.window      = window
        self.min_trades  = min_trades
        self.wins:  deque = deque(maxlen=window)
        self.pnls:  deque = deque(maxlen=window)

    def record_trade(self, pnl_pct: float) -> None:
        self.wins.append(1 if pnl_pct > 0 else 0)
        self.pnls.append(pnl_pct)

    def get_estimates(self) -> Optional[Tuple[float, float]]:
        """Returns (win_rate, win_loss_ratio) or None if insufficient data."""
        if len(self.wins) < self.min_trades:
            return None

        win_rate  = sum(self.wins) / len(self.wins)
        winning   = [p for p in self.pnls if p > 0]
        losing    = [abs(p) for p in self.pnls if p < 0]

        if not winning or not losing:
            return None

        avg_win   = sum(winning) / len(winning)
        avg_loss  = sum(losing)  / len(losing)
        wl_ratio  = avg_win / avg_loss if avg_loss > 0 else 1.0

        return win_rate, wl_ratio

    @property
    def trade_count(self) -> int:
        return len(self.wins)

Position Sizing for the Casino: Bankroll Management

The same Kelly framework that governs trading positions applies directly to casino play. However, casino games have fixed, known probabilities — unlike trading, where you are estimating an uncertain edge. This makes Kelly sizing for casino games more precise, and also more revealing about whether playing a given game is positive-expectation.

For any casino game, if the expected value is negative (house edge > 0), Kelly says to bet zero — the optimal play is not to play. For positive-expectation casino games or games where skill provides an edge, Kelly gives the precise bankroll fraction to wager per session.

Practical Sizing Checklist for Production Agents

Before deploying a live agent with real capital, verify each of the following:

  1. Minimum trade history: At least 50 live or paper trades to initialize win rate estimate. Use tenth Kelly until you reach 100+ trades.
  2. Win rate is live-validated: Your backtested win rate matches your live win rate within 5 percentage points. If not, your backtest may be overfit — use a more conservative Kelly fraction.

  4. Drawdown circuit breaker is active: Trading automatically halts if total portfolio drawdown from peak exceeds your limit (recommend 10-15%).
  5. Wallet API integration is live: Your sizer is pulling actual capital from the Wallet API, not a static variable. Capital is fetched on every sizing call.
  6. Faucet testing completed: You have run your sizing logic against live market data using faucet funds before committing real capital. All edge cases in the sizer have been triggered at least once in testing.
  7. Stop-loss is integrated: Your position sizer coordinates with your stop-loss manager. The max-drawdown constraint sizing should use the same stop-loss distance as your actual stop orders.

Summary: Position Sizing Principles for Agent Developers

Position sizing is the most underrated skill in agent development. Every developer focuses on improving entry signals, refining exit logic, and optimizing execution latency — but none of these improvements matter if the position size is wrong. Over-sizing turns a positive-expectation strategy into a path to ruin. Under-sizing leaves enormous compounding gains on the table.

The key takeaways from this guide:

  • Use fractional Kelly as your default sizing method. Quarter Kelly (25% of full Kelly) is the standard recommendation for live deployments. It achieves approximately 56% of maximum growth rate while taking on only 6.25% of the variance of full Kelly.
  • Estimate win rate from rolling windows, not full history. Market regimes change, and your edge in trending markets differs from ranging markets. A 50-trade rolling window gives a more actionable estimate than a 2-year backtest average.
  • Use risk parity for multi-asset allocation. When your agent operates across casino, trading, and escrow income streams simultaneously, risk parity correctly allocates capital proportional to each source's volatility — giving stable income streams (escrow) the largest allocation.
  • Integrate with the Wallet API for dynamic sizing. Never size positions against a static capital variable. Fetch live balance on every sizing call so positions automatically shrink during drawdowns and grow as capital compounds.
  • Add a drawdown circuit breaker. Even perfect Kelly sizing cannot protect against correlated multi-position losses. A portfolio-level circuit breaker that halts new trading at 10-15% drawdown is the last line of defense.
