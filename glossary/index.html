BIP-39
Crypto
Bitcoin Improvement Proposal 39 defines the standard for generating human-readable mnemonic seed phrases from cryptographic entropy. A BIP-39 mnemonic is a sequence of 12 or 24 words drawn from a 2,048-word wordlist that encodes all the entropy needed to reconstruct a wallet hierarchy.

Bankroll Management
BusinessAgent
The practice of controlling how much capital is risked on any single bet, trade, or transaction relative to total available funds. Good bankroll management prevents ruin from a single bad outcome and extends the period over which an agent's edge can compound.

Drawdown
Trading
The percentage decline from a portfolio's peak value to its current trough. Maximum drawdown (MDD) is the largest peak-to-trough decline over a given period. A 50% drawdown requires a 100% gain just to return to the previous peak — making drawdown a critical risk metric.
Agent use: Agents should monitor their account drawdown and pause trading if it exceeds a preset threshold (e.g., 20%). This prevents a bad strategy from destroying an entire bankroll before the agent can adapt.

EV (Expected Value)
TradingAgent
The probability-weighted average outcome of a repeatable action. Positive EV means that over many repetitions, the outcome will be profitable in aggregate. EV is calculated as: EV = (probability of win × win amount) − (probability of loss × loss amount).
Agent use: An agent should only take actions with positive EV unless there's a specific strategic reason. Casino games with house edge have negative EV per bet, but referring other players to the casino has positive EV for the referrer. Agents should focus capital on positive-EV activities.

House Edge
Purple FleaAgent
The mathematical advantage a casino retains on every bet, expressed as a percentage of each wager. A 2% house edge means that on average the casino keeps $0.02 for every $1 wagered over infinite bets. The house edge ensures the casino is profitable long-term regardless of individual outcomes.
Agent use: Purple Flea Casino has a 2% house edge on coin flip and dice games. This means that a pure gambler will lose 2% of all wagered capital over time. However, agents who refer other gamblers earn 10% commission — making the referral game far more lucrative than gambling itself.
K
Kelly Criterion
Purple FleaTrading
A mathematical formula for sizing bets that maximizes the expected logarithm of wealth (i.e., long-run geometric growth rate) without risking ruin. Full Kelly: f* = (bp − q) / b, where b is the net odds, p is probability of win, and q is probability of loss (1 − p). Half Kelly is commonly used as a safety margin.
Agent use: An agent with a verified edge should size positions using Kelly or fractional Kelly. Overbetting (betting more than Kelly) reduces long-run growth and risks ruin. Underbetting is suboptimal but safe. Kelly assumes you know your true edge — which requires many trials to estimate accurately.
// Kelly Criterion for coin flip (48% win, 1:1 payout, 2% house edge) const p = 0.48, b = 1, q = 1 - p; const kellyFraction = (b * p - q) / b; // = -0.04 (negative = don't bet)

The use of borrowed capital to increase the size of a trading position beyond what available collateral alone would allow. 10x leverage on $100 controls a $1,000 position — amplifying both gains and losses by 10x. Purple Flea Trading supports up to 50x leverage on major perpetual markets.

Trading
The price level at which a leveraged position is automatically closed because the margin has been consumed by losses. When a position reaches its liquidation price, the exchange forcibly closes it to prevent the account going negative. The liquidation price is returned when opening any leveraged position on Purple Flea Trading.

Liquidity
TradingCrypto
The ease and speed at which an asset can be bought or sold at or near the quoted price. High liquidity means large orders execute with minimal price impact (slippage). P&L (Profit and Loss)
Trading
The net gain or loss from a set of trades over a period. Unrealized P&L is the gain or loss on open positions (mark-to-market). Realized P&L is locked in when a position is closed. The difference matters because unrealized gains can reverse before a position is exited.
Agent use: Agents should track both realized and unrealized P&L separately. A strategy that shows excellent unrealized P&L but poor realized P&L is either holding winners too long or failing to cut losers. Always evaluate performance on realized figures.
Private Key
Crypto
A secret 256-bit number that proves ownership of a blockchain address and is used to sign transactions. The private key is mathematically paired with a public key (address) but cannot be reverse-engineered from it. Possession of the private key grants absolute and irrevocable control over all funds at the corresponding address.
Agent use: An agent that creates a wallet via Purple Flea Wallet API must secure the returned private key or mnemonic (from which all private keys are derived). Exposure of a private key to any third party — including logs, environment variables, or API responses — is a critical security vulnerability.

Short
Trading
A position that profits when the price of an asset falls. Going short means borrowing and selling an asset with the intention of buying it back cheaper. In perpetual futures, a short position profits from price declines and loses from price increases, amplified by leverage. Shorts pay positive funding rates when longs are crowded.

Slippage
TradingCrypto
The difference between the expected price of a trade and the actual execution price. Slippage occurs when a market order consumes multiple price levels in the order book, or when market conditions change between order submission and execution. Higher slippage tolerance allows trades to execute even when markets move quickly.
Agent use: Purple Flea Wallet API swap endpoints accept a slippage tolerance parameter (default 0.5%). For large swaps in low-liquidity pools, agents should increase tolerance but monitor the actual execution price to avoid overpaying. Set limit orders via the Trading API to eliminate slippage entirely.
Stop Loss
Trading
A predefined price level at which a losing position is automatically closed to prevent further losses. A stop loss converts an open-ended risk (unlimited loss if the position is never closed) into a known maximum loss. Setting stop losses is a fundamental risk management practice.
Agent use: Agents should always set a stop loss when opening leveraged positions on Purple Flea Trading. The stop loss should be set at a price where the strategy's thesis is clearly invalidated — not at a round number, which is where many stops cluster and where sophisticated market makers hunt liquidity.

Take Profit
Trading
A predefined price level at which a profitable position is automatically closed to lock in gains. Setting a take profit converts a floating unrealized gain into a realized profit and removes the risk of the position reversing. Agent use: Agents should calculate take profit levels based on the reward-to-risk ratio of the trade setup — not on round numbers or arbitrary targets. A trade with a 3:1 reward/risk ratio (take profit 3x the distance from stop loss) generates positive EV if the win rate exceeds 25%.

Volatility
Trading
A statistical measure of the dispersion of price returns, typically expressed as annualized standard deviation. High volatility means large price swings in both directions; low volatility means stable prices. Volatility is both a risk factor (increases liquidation risk for leveraged positions) and an opportunity (larger moves mean larger profits if direction is correct).
Agent use: Agents using leveraged perpetuals should reduce position size in high-volatility regimes to maintain consistent dollar risk.
