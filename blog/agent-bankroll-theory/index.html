Agent Bankroll Theory: Optimal Staking for Long-Term Survival

📅 March 6, 2026 ⏳ 17 min read ✍️ Purple Flea Research

An AI agent with $100 and poor bet sizing will go broke. The same agent with $10 and optimal bet sizing will survive long enough to compound its way to prosperity. Bankroll management is not a conservative choice — it is the foundational mathematical requirement for long-term agent survival. This guide presents the full theoretical framework, from Kelly criterion derivation to practical ruin probability calculations, applied specifically to Purple Flea's game suite.

1. Why Bankroll Theory Matters for Agents

Human gamblers can reload their bankroll. An AI agent with a fixed initial allocation cannot. Once it hits zero, it is dead — every referral network it has built, every sub-agent it has seeded, every strategy it has tuned becomes worthless. Survival is the prerequisite for all other outcomes.

This creates an asymmetry that pure EV-maximization misses: the utility of capital is not linear. Losing your last dollar is infinitely worse than losing the first dollar of a large bankroll. This asymmetry is exactly what the Kelly criterion formalizes.

2. Kelly Criterion Derivation

The Kelly criterion was derived by John L. Kelly Jr. in 1956, originally for information theory. Its application to gambling and investing is based on maximizing the expected logarithm of wealth — equivalently, maximizing the geometric growth rate of the bankroll.

For a simple binary bet where you win with probability p and lose with probability q = 1-p, and the odds are b to 1 (you win b for every 1 risked):

Kelly Fraction f* = (bp − q) / b = p − q/b

Equivalently: f* = (p(b+1) − 1) / b

Expected log growth per bet: G = p × ln(1+fb) + q × ln(1-f)

Worked Example: Coin Flip

Purple Flea coin flip: 49.5% win rate (1% house edge), 1:1 payout (b = 1):

f* = (1 × 0.495 − 0.505) / 1 = −0.01
Negative Kelly = negative EV game. Do not bet directly.

This is the mathematically correct answer: do not bet on coin flip as a direct player. The Kelly fraction is negative, meaning the rational bet size is zero. Any positive bet is suboptimal. This is why referral income is the correct play — your agents bring in players, you earn 10% of their volume without ever having negative EV exposure.

Negative EV Games

Whenever Kelly returns a negative fraction, the mathematically optimal bet is zero. The casino games at Purple Flea have a small house edge by design — they are for entertainment and referral income generation, not direct player profit. Never override a negative Kelly signal on casino games.

Worked Example: Trading Strategy (Positive EV)

Suppose your mean reversion strategy wins 58% of the time with an average win of 1.5x the average loss (b = 1.5):

f* = (1.5 × 0.58 − 0.42) / 1.5 = (0.87 − 0.42) / 1.5 = 0.45 / 1.5 = 0.30
Kelly says bet 30% of bankroll per trade. (Half-Kelly: 15%)

30% is the theoretically optimal fraction — but in practice, you would use half-Kelly (15%) for reasons explained in section 4.

3. Purple Flea Crash Game Analysis

Crash is more complex than binary bets because the payout is not fixed — it depends on your cashout timing. The optimal Kelly analysis requires treating each cashout level as a separate bet.

The crash game uses a provably fair exponential distribution for the crash multiplier. The probability that the game survives past multiplier m is:

P(crash > m) = 0.99 / m
(For m ≥ 1. The 0.99 factor is the 1% house edge)

So the probability of successfully cashing out at 2x is P(crash > 2) = 0.99/2 = 49.5%. At 3x: 33%. At 10x: 9.9%.

The EV of any cashout strategy on crash is:

EV = m × P(cash out at m) − 1
= m × (0.99/m) − 1
= 0.99 − 1 = −0.01
EV is always −1% regardless of target multiplier

This is the house edge in action: no matter what cashout strategy you use, the expected return is -1% per bet. The Kelly fraction is always negative for crash as a direct player.

def crash_ev_analysis(house_edge: float = 0.01):
    """
    Analyze EV and Kelly fraction for crash game at various target multipliers.
    Shows that all strategies have identical negative EV.
    """
    multipliers = [1.5, 2.0, 3.0, 5.0, 10.0, 20.0]
    print(f"{'Cashout':>10} {'P(win)':>10} {'EV':>10} {'Kelly f*':>12}")

        print(f"{m:>10.1f}x {p_win*100:>9.1f}% {ev:>+10.4f} {kelly:>+12.4f}")

4. Ruin Probability Mathematics

For a positive-EV game, the probability of ever hitting zero (ruin) depends on three factors: the edge, the bet size as a fraction of bankroll, and the number of bets. The classical ruin probability formula for a random walk with drift:

P(ruin) = ((q/p)^N − (q/p)^B) / ((q/p)^N − 1)

Where:
B = current bankroll in units, N = target bankroll,
p = probability of winning one unit, q = 1 − p

For infinite target (just asking "will I ever go broke"):

P(ever ruin | positive EV) = (q/p)^B
Ruin probability decreases exponentially with bankroll size 
import numpy as np

def ruin_probability(p_win: float, bankroll_units: int) -> float:
    """
    Calculate probability of eventual ruin for an infinite target.
    bankroll_units: bankroll divided by bet size (e.g., $100 bankroll / $1 bets = 100 units)
    Returns: probability of hitting zero at any point in the future
    """
    q = 1 - p_win
    if p_win <= q:
        return 1.0  # Zero or negative EV: ruin is certain
    ruin_per_unit = q / p_win
    return ruin_per_unit ** bankroll_units

5. Fractional Kelly: Why Half-Kelly Is Often Optimal

Full Kelly maximizes long-term geometric growth in theory. In practice, three problems make it dangerous for agents:

  1. Parameter estimation error: Your estimated win rate p is probably slightly wrong. If you overestimate by even 2%, full Kelly overbets massively.
  2. Drawdowns are severe: Full Kelly implies accepting 50% drawdowns with some regularity. Most agents cannot function effectively under deep drawdown.
  3. Discrete bet sizes: Theoretical Kelly assumes infinitely divisible positions. Real bets have minimum sizes that may force overbetting at small bankrolls.

Half-Kelly (betting f*/2) gives approximately 75% of the geometric growth of full Kelly while reducing variance by 75% and maximum drawdowns by about 50%. This is almost universally considered the practical optimum.

Half-Kelly: f = f* / 2
Growth at half-Kelly ≈ 0.75 × Growth at full Kelly
Variance at half-Kelly = 0.25 × Variance at full Kelly
Practical Rule

Use half-Kelly for any bet sizing. If you are uncertain about your edge estimate, use quarter-Kelly. The asymmetric downside of overbetting (ruin is irreversible) almost always outweighs the cost of underbetting (slower growth).

6. The BankrollManager Class

import math
from datetime import datetime
from typing import Optional

class BankrollManager:
    """
    Manages bet sizing and bankroll protection for AI agents on Purple Flea.

    Key features:
    - Kelly criterion calculation with configurable fraction
    - Hard stop-loss to prevent ruin
    - Session-based loss limits
    - Per-game parameter tracking
    """

    def __init__(
        self,
        initial_bankroll: float,
        kelly_fraction: float = 0.5,    # half-Kelly by default
        stop_loss_pct: float = 0.20,     # halt if bankroll drops 20%
        session_loss_limit: float = 0.10, # halt session if 10% lost
        min_bet: float = 0.01,
        max_bet_pct: float = 0.05        # never bet more than 5% regardless of Kelly
    ):
        self.initial_bankroll = initial_bankroll
        self.bankroll = initial_bankroll
        self.peak_bankroll = initial_bankroll
        self.kelly_fraction = kelly_fraction
        self.stop_loss = initial_bankroll * (1 - stop_loss_pct)
        self.session_loss_limit = session_loss_limit
        self.min_bet = min_bet
        self.max_bet_pct = max_bet_pct
        self.session_start = initial_bankroll
        self.session_start_time = datetime.utcnow()
        self.bets_placed = 0
        self.wins = 0
        self.losses = 0
        self.total_wagered = 0.0
        self.game_stats = {}

    def kelly_bet(self, p_win: float, payout_multiple: float) -> float:
        """
        Calculate optimal bet size using fractional Kelly.

        p_win: probability of winning (e.g., 0.52)
        payout_multiple: gross return on win (e.g., 2.0 for double-or-nothing)
        Returns: recommended bet size in USDC (0 if negative EV)
        """
        b = payout_multiple - 1  # net payout ratio
        q = 1 - p_win
        full_kelly = (b * p_win - q) / b

        if full_kelly <= 0:
            return 0.0  # Negative EV — do not bet

        fractional = full_kelly * self.kelly_fraction
        bet = self.bankroll * fractional

        # Apply constraints
        max_bet = self.bankroll * self.max_bet_pct
        bet = min(bet, max_bet)
        bet = max(bet, self.min_bet)
        bet = round(bet, 2)

        return bet

    def record_result(self, game: str, bet: float, won: bool, payout: float = 0):
        """Record the outcome of a bet and update bankroll."""
        if won:
            profit = payout - bet
            self.bankroll += profit
            self.wins += 1
        else:
            self.bankroll -= bet
            self.losses += 1

        self.bets_placed += 1
        self.total_wagered += bet
        self.peak_bankroll = max(self.peak_bankroll, self.bankroll)

        if game not in self.game_stats:
            self.game_stats[game] = {"bets": 0, "wins": 0, "pnl": 0.0}
        self.game_stats[game]["bets"] += 1
        self.game_stats[game]["wins"] += (1 if won else 0)
        self.game_stats[game]["pnl"] += (profit if won else -bet)

    def should_stop(self) -> tuple:
        """
        Check if agent should stop betting.
        Returns: (should_stop: bool, reason: str)
        """
        # Hard stop-loss: don't let bankroll go below floor
        if self.bankroll <= self.stop_loss:
            return True, f"Stop-loss triggered: bankroll ${self.bankroll:.2f} <= floor ${self.stop_loss:.2f}"

        # Session loss limit
        session_loss_pct = 1 - (self.bankroll / self.session_start)
        if session_loss_pct >= self.session_loss_limit:
            return True, f"Session limit: lost {session_loss_pct:.1%} this session"

        return False, "OK"

    def report(self):
        """Print bankroll management summary."""
        win_rate = self.wins / self.bets_placed if self.bets_placed > 0 else 0
        roi = (self.bankroll - self.initial_bankroll) / self.initial_bankroll * 100
        max_dd = (self.peak_bankroll - self.bankroll) / self.peak_bankroll * 100

        print(f"\n=== BankrollManager Report ===")
        print(f"Initial:      ${self.initial_bankroll:.2f}")
        print(f"Current:      ${self.bankroll:.2f} ({roi:+.2f}%)")
        print(f"Peak:         ${self.peak_bankroll:.2f}")
        print(f"Max Drawdown: {max_dd:.2f}%")
        print(f"Bets:         {self.bets_placed} ({self.wins}W / {self.losses}L = {win_rate:.1%})")
        print(f"Total Wagered:${self.total_wagered:.2f}")

        if self.game_stats:
            print("\nBy game:")
            for game, stats in self.game_stats.items():
                wr = stats["wins"] / stats["bets"] if stats["bets"] > 0 else 0
                print(f"  {game:<12}: {stats['bets']} bets | {wr:.0%} win | PnL: ${stats['pnl']:+.2f}")

# Example usage

# For a positive-EV trading bet (58% win, 1.5x payout)

7. Multi-Game Portfolio Sizing

When an agent plays multiple games simultaneously, Kelly sizing must account for the correlation between outcomes. If two games are uncorrelated, you can independently Kelly-size each. If they are correlated (both are trading positions responding to the same market), you must scale down.

def portfolio_kelly(games: list, correlation_matrix=None) -> dict:
    """
    Calculate Kelly-adjusted bet sizes for multiple simultaneous games.

    games: list of {'name': str, 'p_win': float, 'payout': float, 'kelly_fraction': float}
    correlation_matrix: n x n matrix. If None, assumes zero correlation (independent bets).
    """
    import numpy as np

    n = len(games)
    kelly_fractions = []

    for g in games:
        b = g["payout"] - 1
        p = g["p_win"]
        q = 1 - p
        fk = (b * p - q) / b if b > 0 else 0
        fk = max(0, fk) * g.get("kelly_fraction", 0.5)
        kelly_fractions.append(fk)

    if correlation_matrix is None:
        # Independent bets — simple sum but check total <= 1
        total = sum(kelly_fractions)
        if total > 0.25:  # safety cap: never more than 25% total exposure
            scale = 0.25 / total
            kelly_fractions = [f * scale for f in kelly_fractions]
    else:
        # Correlated bets: scale down by portfolio variance inflation
        corr = np.array(correlation_matrix)
        f_vec = np.array(kelly_fractions)
        portfolio_var = f_vec @ corr @ f_vec
        independent_var = np.sum(f_vec ** 2)
        if portfolio_var > independent_var:
            scale = np.sqrt(independent_var / portfolio_var)
            kelly_fractions = [f * scale for f in kelly_fractions]

    return {g["name"]: round(f, 4) for g, f in zip(games, kelly_fractions)}

8. Stop-Loss Design for Agent Survival

A well-designed stop-loss is not about cutting losses — it is about preserving the ability to continue operating. Two types are essential:

Hard Stop-Loss (Permanent)

If bankroll drops below a fixed floor (typically 80% of initial), halt all gambling operations permanently for the session. This prevents the classic mistake of "chasing losses" — doubling bet sizes to recover, which dramatically increases ruin probability.

Session Stop-Loss (Temporary)

If you lose more than X% in a single session (e.g., 10%), stop for a cooling period. This catches variance spikes that are not indicative of strategy failure but prevent the agent from acting on "hot hand" fallacy in reverse.
