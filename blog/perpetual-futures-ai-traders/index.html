What Are Perpetual Futures?

A perpetual future — or "perp" — is a derivative contract that tracks the price of an underlying asset without an expiration date. Unlike traditional futures contracts that expire quarterly or monthly, perpetual futures can be held indefinitely. They were invented by BitMEX in 2016 and have since become the most traded financial instrument in the crypto ecosystem by volume.

The core mechanic is straightforward: you post collateral (margin), and you control a leveraged position in an underlying asset. If you're long BTC and the price rises, you profit proportionally to your leverage. If it falls, your losses are also magnified — and if your margin falls below the maintenance threshold, your position is liquidated.

What makes perps unique compared to spot trading:

  • Bidirectional: You can profit from falling prices by going short — impossible with spot holdings.
  • Leveraged: Control 10x, 20x, or 50x your capital. A 1% move becomes a 50% gain (or loss) at 50x.
  • No expiry: No need to roll contracts, no basis risk from expiration, no settlement friction.
  • Funding income: When on the correct side of the market, you receive periodic payments from opposing positions.

Mark Price vs Index Price

Every perpetual contract tracks two prices simultaneously. The index price is the reference price sourced from multiple spot exchanges — this is the "true" market price. The mark price is the price used to calculate your unrealized P&L and liquidation threshold. It incorporates the index price plus a fair value premium derived from the order book, smoothed to prevent manipulation.

Your position is never liquidated based on brief wicks in the index price — only sustained moves in the mark price trigger liquidations. This is an important protection for AI agents that might be exposed during high-volatility events.

The Funding Rate Mechanism

The funding rate is the mechanism that keeps perpetual prices anchored to spot prices. Every 8 hours (at 00:00, 08:00, and 16:00 UTC), a payment occurs between all long and all short position holders.

  • When the perpetual trades above spot (bullish sentiment): longs pay shorts. The funding rate is positive.
  • When the perpetual trades below spot (bearish sentiment): shorts pay longs. The funding rate is negative.

Funding rates are expressed as a percentage per 8-hour period. Annualize by multiplying by 3 (payments per day) × 365 (days per year) = 1095×. A 0.01% per-8h rate is approximately 10.95% annualized. During extreme bull markets, rates can spike to 0.3% per 8h — over 300% annualized flowing from longs to shorts.

Key Insight

The funding rate is the most important number for AI agents. Unlike price prediction (hard), funding rates are directly observable and their direction is predictable from sentiment data. An AI agent can earn funding income with zero directional risk using delta-neutral strategies.

Funding Rate Mechanics in Depth

Understanding the funding rate formula is essential for building strategies around it.

The Funding Rate Formula

The funding rate is calculated from two components: the interest rate (fixed at 0.01% per 8h, representing the cost of leverage) and the premium index (the deviation of perpetual price from spot index price).

funding_rate = clamp(premium_index + interest_rate, -0.75%, +0.75%) # Where: premium_index = (mark_price - index_price) / index_price interest_rate = 0.0001 # 0.01% fixed per 8h # Payment amount for your position: funding_payment = position_size * mark_price * funding_rate # Positive rate: longs pay this amount to shorts # Negative rate: shorts pay this amount to longs

Funding Rate Arbitrage Strategy

Funding rate arbitrage — often called "cash and carry" in perp markets — is the flagship delta-neutral strategy for AI agents. The mechanics are:

  1. Identify a perpetual market with a high positive funding rate (e.g., 0.08% per 8h).
  2. Buy the equivalent amount of the underlying asset on spot (or hold it).
  3. Short the perpetual at 1x leverage (no additional leverage needed).
  4. Your spot position and short perp cancel each other's price exposure — you are delta-neutral.
  5. Every 8 hours, you collect funding payments from longs (who are paying to maintain their bullish positions).
  6. Close both legs when the funding rate normalizes.

Risk Management for Perpetual Agents

Perpetual futures carry risks that spot trading does not. Liquidation — the forced closure of your position when margin falls to zero — is the primary danger. A good risk framework for AI agents addresses four dimensions.

1. Position Sizing

The Kelly criterion is the mathematically optimal framework for sizing positions. For perpetual trading, a simplified conservative version:

# Simplified Kelly for perp trading def kelly_size(win_rate: float, avg_win: float, avg_loss: float, portfolio: float) -> float: edge = win_rate * avg_win - (1 - win_rate) * avg_loss variance = win_rate * avg_win**2 + (1 - win_rate) * avg_loss**2 full_kelly = edge / variance # Use half-Kelly for safety margin fraction = full_kelly * 0.5 return min(fraction * portfolio, portfolio * 0.10) # Cap at 10% per position # Example: strategy with 58% win rate, avg win $120, avg loss $80 size = kelly_size(0.58, 120, 80, 10000) # Returns ~$412

2. Leverage Rules

  • Delta-neutral strategies: 1x leverage only. You're earning carry, not making directional bets.
  • Directional strategies: Maximum 5x. Higher leverage should only be used by agents with extensive backtesting.
  • Never use 50x leverage on automated agents. A 2% adverse move liquidates you completely.
  • Cross margin vs isolated margin: Use isolated margin so a single bad position cannot liquidate your entire portfolio.

3. Max Drawdown Rules

Your agent must track drawdown from peak portfolio value and enforce hard stops:

MAX_DAILY_DRAWDOWN = 0.05 # Stop all trading if down 5% in a day MAX_PORTFOLIO_DRAWDOWN = 0.15 # Halt and alert if down 15% from peak CASH_RESERVE = 0.30 # Always keep 30% of portfolio in cash/stables

4. Correlation Between Positions

BTC and ETH are highly correlated (0.85+ correlation). Holding both long is not diversification — it doubles your directional exposure. Your agent should check correlation before opening new positions:

import numpy as np def portfolio_is_overconcentrated(returns_matrix: np.ndarray, threshold: float = 0.7) -> bool: """Returns True if any pair of positions is too correlated.""" corr = np.corrcoef(returns_matrix) n = corr.shape[0] for i in range(n): for j in range(i+1, n): if corr[i, j] > threshold: return True return False
Critical Risk Warning

Liquidation cascades are the greatest risk for perpetual trading agents. During extreme volatility events, cascading liquidations can move prices 20-30% in minutes. Your agent must have hard-coded position size limits, stop-losses that cannot be overridden, and a circuit breaker that halts all new positions when volatility exceeds 3 standard deviations from normal.
